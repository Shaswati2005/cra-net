 A B S T R A C T
Gait is the manner or pattern of walking governed by the coordination of muscles, joints, and the nervous 
system. Abnormal gait can affect general mobility and quality of life by stressing muscle joints and raising the 
chance of falling. Gait analysis poses several challenges, which include inter-class similarities and variations in 
walking patterns among individuals with the same pathology. The limited dataset and class imbalance further 
hinder the performance of the model. Earlier vision-based systems analyzing gait to detect these abnormalities 
were often limited to binary classification, distinguishing only between normal and abnormal. Later, some 
advanced systems attempted to identify specific pathologies. However, these models could not capture subtle 
variations in gait patterns, reducing overall performance. To address this issue, a novel network, cross 
reverse attention network (CRA-Net), which incorporates two complementary attention mechanisms called 
cross reverse attention block (CRAB) and cross attention block (CAB) to capture inter-class variations better, 
is proposed. CRAB adopts an inverse attention mask to extract complex features from discriminative regions 
and integrate them into features extracted by traditional Convolutional neural networks (CNN), making the 
model robust. CAB is designed to integrate diverse feature sets from local and global layers, thereby balancing 
multi-scale feature integration. Concatenating features from three levels of the proposed architecture makes 
the model learn all relevant features that aid in classifying gait pathologies. The experimental results have 
shown that CRA-Net effectively captures variations in gait patterns, achieving superior performance compared 
to existing methods.
1. Introduction
Gait is typically defined as a cyclical series of limb movements 
that facilitate human locomotion. Various disorders [1,2] impair this 
cycle, leading to abnormal gait patterns that differ from normal move-
ment in parameters such as walking speed, joint angles, step or stride 
length, and foot pressure. The nervous system is critical in control-
ling movement and coordination, so walking abnormalities are often 
linked to neurological conditions. Recent technological advancements 
have transformed gait analysis [3,4] into a valuable tool for medical 
assessments, particularly in diagnosing an individual’s health, identity, 
and walking patterns [5,6].
Gait analysis mainly relies on vision-based and sensor-based meth-
ods. Vision-based techniques use cameras to record or take pictures of

Deep learning offers effective methods for automatically extracting 
features from unprocessed data. By learning structured representations, 
deep learning models like CNN [12] show a remarkable ability to 
extract complex patterns from images. These multilayer networks are 
efficient for image classification since each layer captures more abstract 
information like edges, textures, and objects. CNN is widely applied 
in numerous domains, such as object recognition [13] and medical 
image analysis [14]. Similarly, applying transfer learning techniques 
for these tasks is common, particularly when datasets are limited. 
Pre-trained models like VGG16 and VGG19 [15,16] are widely used. 
Though these techniques boost efficiency, they may experience over-
fitting when trained on small-scale datasets, as the model does not 
perform well when applied to task-specific data. Also, recent advance-
ments in hierarchical and progressive learning [17] enhance classifi-
cation by extracting features at multiple abstraction levels and refining 
details gradually. These methods address challenges like low-resolution 
inputs and class imbalances. Key point sensitive loss further improves 
feature separability, aiding the recognition of underrepresented pat-
terns, which are applied in sonar image classification. Nowadays, deep 
learning models increasingly incorporate attention mechanisms [18,19] 
for classification tasks. Attention can assist the model in focusing on 
important features that are essential for finding anomalies in motion. 
Unlike transfer learning, which depends on standard features from 
unrelated tasks, these mechanisms can learn to prioritize task-specific 
aspects. This makes attention mechanisms very useful for tough clas-
sification tasks. The proposed CRA-Net model overcomes challenges 
in identifying specific pathologies through CRAB, which creates an 
inverse attention mask that enhances subtle variations by suppressing 
non-discriminative features, ensuring even minor abnormalities are 
highlighted. CAB preserves spatial dependencies by allowing multi-
scale feature interactions, preventing information loss and improving 
robustness to class imbalances. By combining CRAB and CAB, these 
processes improve feature representations, resulting in the diagnosis of 
more precise gait abnormalities.
The contributions of the proposed work can be summarized as 
follows :
• The cross-reverse attention block (CRAB) employs a reverse atten-
tion mechanism that revisits less prominent or neglected details 
to enhance feature representation and ensure finer feature re-
finement. CRAB captures cross-dimensional feature interactions 
to improve the model’s understanding of complex relationships 
within the data.
• The proposed cross attention block (CAB), integrates features 
from various local and global layers to capture cross-correlation 
between the features of complementary attention networks.
• The proposed architecture combines two complementary atten-
tion mechanisms, CRAB and CAB, to focus on important and less 
obvious gait patterns from various layers, improving its ability to 
differentiate between normal and pathological gaits.
• The proposed model delivers a more computationally efficient 
network while utilizing fewer trainable parameters than tradi-
tional CNN mechanisms.
The contributions of this work lie in integrating CRAB and CAB atten-
tion mechanisms with convolution layers. These contributions collec-
tively improve the accuracy of the proposed model in the pathological 
gait classification.

2. Related works
Everyday living depends greatly on walking, and gait analysis is es-
sential for the early identification and detection of a number of medical 
disorders, which enhances the effectiveness of treatment. Gait analysis 
is used in rehabilitation programs to monitor the effects of treatment. 
With an emphasis on clinical gait assessment, the study [4] exam-
ines various methods of gait analysis. It shows how early subjective 
techniques gave way to more realistic vision-based strategies.
2.1. Deep learning
Deep learning techniques popularly assist in the classification of 
gait disorders and help in extracting significant features from gait 
sequences. CNNs examine the silhouette patterns, accurately discrim-
inating between normal and abnormal gaits.
Nieto et al. [20] used a computer vision-based feature extraction 
method, selecting legs-angle time series and gait energy image (GEI) 
features to classify gait pathologies. Results showed that both fea-
tures distinguished normal from abnormal gait; however, the legs-angle 
had difficulty differentiating hemiplegic from diplegic and neuropathic 
from regular due to similar patterns. To improve classification ac-
curacy across a range of gait disorders, the study [15] introduced 
a novel method that used GEI and VGG-19-based feature extraction 
with transfer learning. The proposed method outperformed traditional 
approaches. Similarly, the study [21] suggests the skeleton energy 
image (SEI), a condensed representation that emphasizes mobility and 
reduces dependency on body structure. The proposed method created 
a new pathological gait dataset for community usage and improved 
classification performance by merging GEI and SEI. A variable step-size 
conjugate gradient technique was utilized to integrate data from three 
simulated abnormal gaits. The method pre-processes data using a gait 
information localization approach to facilitate classification through 
efficient feature extraction and temporal relevance handling. Similarly, 
using silhouettes in sagittal views from three datasets (INIT, DAI, and 
DAI 2), an improved CNN was created in the study [22], which showed 
stability and efficient generalization, with substantial advantages with 
adjusted optimizer, batch size, and epochs. GEI, meanwhile, is of-
ten confused with diplegic and parkinsonian, and hemiplegic, with 
neuropathic gait, due to similar silhouettes. Gao et al. [23] presents 
LCSWnet, an algorithm that combines long short-term memory (LSTM) 
and CNN layers. Similarly, the study [24] used the CNN-LSTM model 
that uses Kinect-recorded skeleton data to categorize multi-class gait 
patterns across nine walking types. These results demonstrate Kinect’s 
potential for automated, non-invasive gait evaluation in assisted living 
and clinical settings.
Zeng et al. [25] presented a video-based gait analysis that uses 
a skeleton-silhouette fusion network to quantify Parkinson’s disease-
related gait parameters such as velocity and arm swing. Skeletal-based 
gait categorization is now possible because of recent developments 
in depth sensors and skeleton tracking, although current techniques 
mainly deal with basic gait patterns. By concentrating on leg joints, Jun 
et al. [26] developed a GRU-based classifier that classified different gait 
styles by combining plantar foot pressure and 3D skeleton data. The 
results show the GRU classifier’s versatility in responding to joint data 
restrictions and its potential to support medicinal properties. Similarly, 
Jun et al. [27] proposed a model that addresses noise and unnecessary 
information in skeleton-based gait data by providing an automatic 
feature extraction method that employs RNN-based autoencoders (AEs), 
specifically LSTM and GRU AEs. Cola et al. [28] proposed an ap-
proach for continuous, unsupervised gait monitoring using a single 
waist-mounted accelerometer to support fall prevention and mobility 
assessments in older patients. This method detects minor gait irregu-
larities using real-time, acceleration-based characteristics. Similarly, Li 
et al. [29] proposed a classification system for gait problems caused by 
neurodegenerative diseases such as Parkinson’s and Hemiplegia based

2.2. Attention
Attention mechanisms in deep learning enable models to focus 
on essential input data elements while dynamically prioritizing more 
relevant features to the task. This enhances performance by increasing 
the model’s capacity to detect key patterns, particularly in sequential 
or spatial data.
Xia et al. proposed a dual-modal deep learning approach [30] 
to evaluate gait in Parkinson’s disease (PD) patients, allowing for 
binary classification of PD and normal gaits and severity evaluation. 
The model employs CNNs and attention-enhanced LSTMs based on 
segmented vertical ground response force signals for left and right 
gait modeling. This resulted in excellent classification accuracy and 
a dependable, quantitative tool for PD gait evaluation. Similarly, a 
lightweight vision-based system [31] was proposed by Zhou et al. for 
stroke therapy and provides real-time gait assessment on mobile de-
vices, allowing for home-based monitoring of hemiplegia recovery. The 
system achieved good classification accuracy for hemiplegia severity 
levels with minimal computing needs using SEI and an attention-based 
CNN. A convolved self-attention neural network for wearable IMU 
sensor-based gait detection and human activity recognition (HAR) [32] 
is proposed by Tai et al. The model uses a simplified, fully connected 
layer to minimize parameters and embeds a convolved window in the 
self-attention module to use previous time steps. This method outper-
forms previous state-of-the-art models by achieving excellent accuracy 
with a small network size.
A 2D video-based technique with posture estimation [33] is pro-
posed by Tang et al. to differentiate between the gait patterns of 
children with developmental coordination disorder (DCD), early onset 
ataxia (EOA), and healthy controls (HC). The model achieves a better 
f1-score in group-level classification using an attention-based graph 
convolutional network (A-GCN) with channel-wise and temporal at-
tention, showing promise for more effective and objective diagnosis 
in child movement disorders. Tian et al. employed spatio-temporal 
attention enhanced gait-structural graph convolutional network(AGS-
GCN) [34] framework for abnormal gait recognition to extract fine-
grained gait properties from skeletal data. This model performed well 
on abnormal gait datasets by improving representation and general-
ization by enhancing critical joint features. Similarly, a two-stream 
spatial–temporal attention graph convolution network (2s-ST-AGCN) 
[35] is proposed by Guo et al. for automated evaluation of the gait 
of people with Parkinson’s disease (PD). It models the spatial and 
temporal dynamics of motor disorders using video skeletal sequences. 
This approach is promising as a distant, objective PD evaluation tool in 
clinical practice, as it effectively captures discriminative gait variables 
under intense supervision and multi-scale attention.
The attention module effectively builds global dependencies among 
features, enhancing image clarity. This is demonstrated by the proposed 
cross-view gait recognition model [36], which combines a multi-scale 
feature residual module (MFRM) and self-attention (SA) mechanism 
within a GAN framework. The MFRM extracts both local and global fea-
tures, while the SA module strengthens them. Similarly, Chen et al. [37] 
proposed the gait pyramid attention network (GPAN), which combines 
local pyramid attention with global attention fusion Learning to focus 
on key gait features across several receptive fields for gait recognition.
Though attention mechanisms focus on essential information, they 
may not observe inter-class variations and co-relations between feature 
sets, which must be considered for classification tasks.
3. Methodology
3.1. Overview of the proposed architecture
Fig.  1 shows the proposed model’s architecture for classifying 
gait pathologies. The proposed approach is a multi-level, attention-
enhanced architecture intended to classify pathological gait data effi-
ciently. It combines two complementary mechanisms, CRAB and CAB 
modules, with convolution layers, allowing the model to highlight the 
key features required flexibly. The architecture is separated into several 
levels (block I, II, and III) that gradually integrate different types of 
features from each level. Convolution and pooling layers are incor-
porated throughout each model’s level to extract hierarchical features 
gradually. Skip connections guarantee improved information retention 
by enabling feature reuse across levels. The aggregated features are 
processed through numerous layers to generate the final classification 
by multi-level feature extraction.

4.1.1. INIT gait dataset
The INIT gait dataset [38] contains side-view sequences of high-
quality binary silhouettes of 10 healthy people (9 males, one female) 
exhibiting diverse abnormal walking types. The dataset was collected 
in a controlled studio environment with a green chroma background. It 
includes seven simulated impaired gait styles inspired by neurological

4.3.3. Evaluation of class-specific metrics
The class-wise evaluation results of the proposed model using stan-
dard performance metrics like accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score 
are mentioned in this section. Accuracy is a fundamental measure, 
indicating the overall correctness of the model’s predictions across 
all classes. To further analyze the model’s performance, precision and 
recall are employed. Precision assesses the model’s ability to minimize 
false positives, ensuring that the positive predictions are accurate. At 
the same time, recall evaluates its effectiveness in identifying all true 
positive cases, which is critical in medical applications. The F1-score, 
as the harmonic mean of precision and recall, combines these metrics 
into a single value, providing a balanced measure that is particularly 
useful in scenarios with imbalanced class distributions.
The overall class-wise evaluation metrics for both INIT and GAIT-IT 
datasets are summarized in Tables  4 & 5, respectively.

played in Fig.  4, which visualizes the model’s classification perfor-
mance. It shows correct predictions along the diagonal and high-
lights misclassifications off the diagonal, offering valuable insights into 
specific error patterns.
4.3.4. Comparison with different existing models
To evaluate the performance of the proposed model, a comparison 
is made with several state-of-the-art techniques shown in Table  6. The 
comparison is based on classification accuracy, number of parame-
ters, QWK (Quadratic Weighted Kappa) for agreement measurement, 
GFLOPs (Giga Floating Point Operations per second) for computational 
complexity and inference time for efficiency and speed. QWK is a sta-
tistical metric that measures agreement between predicted and actual 
classifications. GFLOPs measure the computational complexity of the 
model by counting the number of floating-point operations required for 
inference. In contrast, inference time refers to the time the model takes 
to process and classify a gait sample. The authors in [40] introduced 
the VGG16 model, a deep CNN utilizing small 3 × 3 filters, achieving an 
accuracy of 89.56% on the INIT gait dataset and 91.89% on the GAIT-
IT dataset. The VGG19 model [40], an extended version of VGG16 
with additional layers, resulted in an accuracy of 91.39% on the INIT 
gait dataset and 90.47% on the GAIT-IT dataset. CBAM (Convolutional 
Block Attention Module) [41] enhanced feature representation using 
channel and spatial attention mechanisms, improving accuracy and 
QWK. Similarly, the authors in [42] proposed Squeeze-and-Excitation 
network, which incorporates channel-wise refinement, achieved an 
accuracy of 95.86% on the INIT gait dataset and 97.67% on the 
GAIT-IT dataset. Also, models by authors in [43,44] are compared 
with the proposed model. An attention-based lightweight CNN [31] 
incorporating Ghostnet [45] is also trained over INIT gait and GAIT-
IT datasets, achieving accuracies of 95.39% and 98.82%, respectively. 
While GhostNet [45] has lower GFLOPs, its inference time is slightly 
higher compared to CRA-Net. The proposed CRA-Net outperformed all 
other techniques in classification, achieving an accuracy of 99.66% 
on the INIT gait dataset and 99.16% on the GAIT-IT dataset, while 
also demonstrating lower GFLOPs and faster inference time, making 
it efficient computationally efficient model and establishing itself as a 
relatively best-performing model with fewer parameters.
5. Conclusion
In this study, a novel approach for the classification of gait patholo-
gies was introduced using the proposed CRA-Net. The model incorpo-
rates two complementary attention mechanisms, CRAB and CAB, to 
address the limitations of previous models in capturing inter-class vari-
ations in gait patterns. CRAB employs inverse attention masks to focus 
on complex and discriminative regions, enhancing feature extraction 
beyond the capabilities of CNNs. On the other hand, CAB integrates 
diverse feature sets from local and global layers, ensuring balanced, 
multi-scale feature representation. The concatenation of features across 
multiple levels within the proposed CRA-Net architecture further en-
hanced its ability to learn all relevant features crucial for accurately 
classifying pathologies. Experimental results demonstrated that the 
CRA-Net effectively captures variations in gait patterns, outperforming 
existing methods in accuracy and robustness. This work can be used for 
early detection and monitoring of gait-related abnormalities. However, 
this model only considers spatial data. Future work can incorporate 
temporal data along with spatial data.
