\begin{abstract}
We classify pathological gait from side-view binary silhouettes and evaluate
only on people the model has never seen. Starting from a frame-level attention
network, we went through nine stages: a Swin-transformer variant, a
spatio-temporal attention model (ST-CRANet), a compact (2+1)D network, a corrected
subject-exclusive protocol, phase-aligned gait cycles (ST-PhaseNet), and the
AnatoPhaseNet family (v1--v6). The final model aligns each gait cycle to nine
clinical phases, encodes it with an ImageNet-inflated (2+1)D ResNet-18 with
circular temporal convolutions, pools six body strips with a half-cycle symmetry
descriptor, and adds a posture-geometry branch, a severity head and a
multi-cycle walk head. With 5.0\,M parameters it reaches 95.47\,\% cycle and
96.28\,\% walk accuracy on GAIT-IT and 92--93\,\% on INIT (6 classes) under
10-fold subject-exclusive cross-validation.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Pathological gait, silhouettes, (2+1)D CNN, gait phases, subject-independent
evaluation, GAIT-IT, INIT.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================
Parkinson's disease, cerebral palsy (diplegia), stroke (hemiplegia) and
neuropathy each change the way a person walks~\cite{baker2018}. A single
side-view camera with silhouette extraction is a cheap, privacy-preserving
screening aid. We use GAIT-IT~\cite{albuquerque2021} (21 subjects, 828
sequences, five gait types at two severity levels) and INIT~\cite{ortells2018}
(10 subjects, 160 sequences, eight styles: normal, reduced left/right arm swing
at two levels, half left/right leg step, full-body impairment; the 6-class
protocol merges the two arm levels).

\textbf{Objectives.} (1) Classify a gait cycle of an \emph{unseen} subject;
(2) give a walk-level decision; (3) estimate severity (normal/mild/severe);
(4) train on a 4\,GB laptop GPU. Every number in this report is the mean over
10 subject-exclusive folds: fold $k$ tests on subject group $k$, selects the
checkpoint on group $k{+}1$ and trains on the rest, with a fixed split seed, so
all models are tested on exactly the same people.

Three lessons shaped the final design. \emph{(i) Protocol first:} in GAIT-IT
the same person performs all five gaits, so any split that is not by person
measures recognition of the \emph{person}. \emph{(ii) Phase alignment:} for a
walk at speed $\alpha$, $x_\alpha(t)=x(\alpha t)$, phase events move to
$\tau_p/\alpha$, so sampling at the phases gives
$x_\alpha(\tau_p/\alpha)=x(\tau_p)$ --- the same nine inputs at any cadence,
and $4.6\times$ more samples (3813 cycles). \emph{(iii) Audit before adding:}
the v4 gains came from fixing defects (e.g.\ \texttt{\_back} walks are mirror
images of \texttt{\_front} walks: correlation 0.65 raw, 0.996 after a flip), not
from a bigger network.

Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} shows the system as nine blocks.

\textbf{B1 -- Cycle extraction and canonicalisation.} Turns a walk into gait
cycles. GAIT-IT provides key-frame annotations; for INIT, which has none, the
cycle period is the autocorrelation peak (40--110 frames) of the bounding-box
aspect ratio, and cycles are cut at alternate step peaks. Every right-to-left
walk is mirrored, so all cycles walk the same way and the network does not
learn each class twice.

\textbf{B2 -- Phase sampler.} Picks the nine key frames of a cycle (initial
contact \dots\ cycle completion), with $\pm2$-frame jitter during training.
Each frame is cropped to the silhouette's bounding box and resized to $96^2$,
which enlarges the limbs from about 35 to 85 pixels. Each phase gets three
channels: the silhouette $x_t$, its cyclic motion $x_t-x_{t-1}$ and its
deviation from the cycle's mean silhouette $x_t-\bar x$.

\textbf{B3 -- (2+1)D backbone.} ResNet-18~\cite{he2016resnet} (stem and
layers 1--3) with every conv and BN layer copied from ImageNet; each 2D kernel
becomes a $1{\times}k{\times}k$ kernel applied to every phase. Each block adds a
temporal branch $\mathbf{v}=\mathrm{ReLU}(\mathbf{u}+\mathrm{BN}_t(W_t\circledast
\mathbf{u}))$~\cite{tran2018r21d} whose BN starts at $\gamma=0$, so the network
starts as the exact pretrained 2D ResNet and learns motion on top. The temporal
convolution is \emph{circular}, $(W\circledast\mathbf{u})_t=\sum_j
W_j\mathbf{u}_{(t+j)\bmod 9}$, because the phase after cycle completion is
initial contact again; no phase is treated as an edge. Layer 4 is dropped to
keep a $6\times6$ map.

\textbf{B4 -- Body-part pooling.} Splits the $6\times6$ map into six horizontal
strips (head to feet) plus one global part~\cite{chao2019gaitset}; each is
average- and max-pooled and projected to 128-d per phase. Diseases affect
different body parts, and each part gets its own features.

\textbf{B5 -- Part temporal head.} Adds a learnable phase embedding, applies a
circular temporal convolution per part, and summarises the nine phases three
ways: attention pooling (which phases matter), max pooling (the strongest
response), and a half-cycle symmetry term. In a side view, half a cycle later
the other leg is in the same pose, so for a symmetric walker
$\mathbf{s}_p=\frac14\sum_{t<4}|\mathbf{f}_{p,t}-\mathbf{f}_{p,t+4}|\approx0$;
one-sided impairment (hemiplegia) makes it large. Output: 384-d per part.

\textbf{B6 -- Geometry branch.} Cropping each frame to a fixed height erases the
body's height over time, which carries crouch, stoop and vertical bob. B6
measures nine signals on the raw silhouette \emph{before} cropping: height,
width, lower- and upper-body width, head bob, centroid height, trunk lean,
forward mass offset and fill ratio. Lengths are divided by the walk's median
height $\tilde h$, so $s h_t/(s\tilde h)=h_t/\tilde h$ is independent of camera
distance $s$. A two-layer circular temporal CNN encodes them into a 192-d
feature and a classifier.

\textbf{B7 -- Classifier ensemble.} One small classifier per part plus the
geometry classifier; their logits are averaged. Averaging $P$ classifiers with
correlation $\rho$ gives variance $\sigma^2(\rho+\frac{1-\rho}{P})\le\sigma^2$,
which replaced v3's 3\,M-parameter head.

\textbf{B8 -- Severity head.} A linear layer on $g$ (all part features plus
geometry, 2880-d) predicts normal / mild / severe; the labels come free with
the data and make $g$ encode \emph{how much} a gait deviates.

\textbf{B9 -- Walk head.} During training, $K\in\{2..6\}$ consecutive cycles
of one walk are embedded ($\mathbf e_k\in\R^{256}$) and the walk is classified
from their mean $\boldsymbol\mu$, standard deviation $\boldsymbol\sigma$ and
mean successive difference $\frac1{K-1}\sum_k|\mathbf e_{k+1}-\mathbf e_k|$, a
learned stride-variability marker. At test time a walk takes the mean
log-probability of its cycles.

\textbf{Training.} Loss $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{focal}}+0.5\,
\mathcal{L}_{\text{part}}+0.3\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{sev}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{walk}}$
(focal $\gamma=1.5$, label smoothing 0.05); square-root class-balanced
sampling; AdamW (lr $10^{-3}$, backbone $0.3\times$, weight decay 0.05), 20
epochs, batch 16, weight EMA 0.98, 3-view jitter test-time averaging; RTX 3050
Laptop, 4\,GB.

\textbf{Progress over our models.} Table~\ref{tab:prog} lists every model on
the same folds. The two largest steps are phase alignment (ST-PhaseNet, from a
model that failed even with shared subjects to 89.1\,\% on unseen ones) and
anatomical cropping (v2, $+2.3$ points). The v4 redesign mainly made results
stable across people: fold std fell from 5.38 to 4.04 and fold 3 rose from 79.9
to 90.9\,\% (Table~\ref{tab:folds}). v5 raised walk accuracy by 1.1 points and
v6 is best on every summary metric, with fewer parameters than ST-CRANet
(5.0 vs.\ 6.6\,M).

\textbf{Ablations.} Each comparison changes one block on the same folds.
\emph{B3:} a 2D ResNet with a cyclic temporal shift~\cite{lin2019tsm} instead of
(2+1)D gives 94.04 vs.\ 94.05\,\%, so v4's gain comes from B1, B4, B5 and B7,
not from 3D convolution. \emph{B8:} removing the severity head leaves cycle
accuracy unchanged (94.76 vs.\ 94.81\,\%) while severity accuracy drops from
79.59 to 35.96\,\% (chance), so severity comes at no cost. \emph{B6:} adding
geometry raises cycle accuracy by 0.66 points and lowers the fold std by 0.52.
\emph{Subject-adversarial training} (gradient reversal~\cite{ganin2015} on $g$)
gave no gain (95.16\,\%) and is off by default.

\textbf{Per class.} Normal, hemiplegic, neuropathic and Parkinsonian recall are
$\ge96.5\,\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:recall}, Fig.~\ref{fig:cm}). Hemiplegic recall is
high from v4 on, when the half-cycle symmetry term (B5) was introduced. The main
remaining error is diplegic$\leftrightarrow$Parkinsonian (71 and 43 cycles):
both are bilateral, symmetric and flexed, so symmetry cannot separate them.
Posture geometry (B6) partly can: diplegic recall rose from 84.0 to 87.9\,\%
and diplegic$\rightarrow$Parkinsonian errors fell from 91 to 71.

\textbf{Subject dependence.} Folds 3 and 9 are the weakest for every version;
their errors follow the walking style of those test subjects, and adversarial
identity removal did not fix them (fold 9: 87.4$\rightarrow$86.3\,\%).

\textbf{INIT.} All versions reach 92--94\,\% on 6 classes
(Table~\ref{tab:init}); differences are 1--2 test cycles per fold. Geometry
helps severity (+1.3 points) but not class accuracy, since INIT's classes
differ in arm swing and step length rather than posture. Most errors are
reduced-arm-swing cycles predicted as normal (9 and 11 cycles,
Fig.~\ref{fig:init}): a missing arm swing changes only a few pixels in front of
the trunk. On 8 classes v4 reaches $85.56\pm8.19\,\%$; recall of the two
half-swing classes is only 58--63\,\%, as ``half'' differs between people.

\textbf{Training behaviour.} Validation uses EMA weights (decay 0.98). With
plain EMA, initialised from the untrained model, INIT validation stayed at
28--33\,\% until epoch 6 (loss $\approx\ln 6$, i.e.\ uniform predictions)
because INIT has only $\sim$29 steps per epoch (GAIT-IT: $\sim$190). Ramping
the decay as $\min(0.98,\frac{1+n}{10+n})$ removes this delay
(Fig.~\ref{fig:curves}c). On GAIT-IT, validation accuracy on unseen subjects
reaches 92.6\,\% by epoch 4 and levels off at about 95.5\,\%
(Fig.~\ref{fig:curves}a); validation loss flattens after epoch 6 and does not
rise again, so the model does not overfit within 20 epochs, unlike ST-CRANet
(100\,\% train vs.\ 77\,\% validation). Training accuracy and cross-entropy stay
on the worse side of validation because training inputs are jittered, erased
and noised, while validation uses clean inputs, averaged weights and
three-view TTA. The warm-up changes test accuracy by +0.66 points on INIT-6
($93.05\pm4.37\,\%$, walk 92.50\,\%) and $-1.07$ on GAIT-IT
($94.39\pm4.74\,\%$, lower in 6 of 10 folds), both within one fold standard
deviation; we keep plain EMA for the reported results.

\textbf{Limitations.} Both datasets contain \emph{simulated} pathologies, and
all results come from one training seed.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
% =====================================================================
Over nine stages we moved from frame-level attention networks that memorised
people to AnatoPhaseNet v6, which reaches 95.47\,\% cycle and 96.28\,\% walk
accuracy on unseen GAIT-IT subjects with 5.0\,M parameters, and 92--93\,\% on
INIT-6. The largest gains came from aligning cycles to clinical phases, from
anatomical cropping and part pooling, and from fixing data and transfer
defects. Next we will run multiple seeds, target the diplegic--Parkinsonian
confusion and the two weak folds (e.g.\ learned bilateral cross-attention
between the two half-cycles, multi-scale part features for thin arms), add
phase-level saliency maps, and test across datasets.
